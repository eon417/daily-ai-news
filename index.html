Claude traces the NIST P-curve seeds to lrand48 and 1997-98 Unix timestamps in under 30 minutes, closing a 25-year question about an NSA backdoor

●●●●● 5/5

Anthropic's 10-16 research post says an internal research model, given the problem without the long-standing 'forgotten passphrase' theory, found in under half an hour that the seeds for NIST P-192, P-224, P-256, P-384 and P-521 are plain outputs of the C library's lrand48 generator seeded with Unix timestamps between May 1997 and April 1998. The P-256 seed is the 1,842nd 20-byte candidate in its run; the binary-curve seeds map to 17 April 1998 once the clock value is doubled, suggesting the program halved the time. Anthropic stresses this is not a cryptographic break or new attack and that the mundane process makes a deliberate backdoor much less plausible, though candidate 876 in the P-256 run also yields a usable curve and nobody knows why it was skipped. Still unexplained: the B-163 seed and the base points for P-224 through P-521. The $36,864 bounty that Filippo Valsorda ran for three years goes to the Internet Security Research Group. Bitcoin's secp256k1 is unaffected; CoinDesk and the crypto press covered the result on 10-17.

  • Seeds = lrand48 output from 1997-98 timestamps; P-256 at iteration 1,842
  • Not a weakness; backdoor now much less plausible. No action for users
  • B-163 seed, several base points and the skipped candidate remain open

Claude Mythos 5 designs quantum circuits that beat published best by up to 17x in gate count, with Lean proofs and 403M sampled decodings as verification

●●●●○ 4/5

A 10-16 Anthropic research post, led by STEM Fellow Andi Gu with Harvard's Susanne Yelin and Mikhail Lukin (also QuEra), gave Claude Mythos 5 plain-language specs for eight quantum circuit primitives across chemistry, optimization, physics simulation and number theory. In a closed loop, Claude wrote circuits in Python, an automated evaluator checked correctness and scored qubits times gates, and Claude revised without human input between rounds. For elliptic-curve point addition, the core step of quantum discrete-log attacks, Claude spent 53 hours developing a reversible divsteps approach that stores no branch history; one variant uses 12% more qubits but 17x fewer gates than the smallest prior design. Its Berlekamp-Massey Reed-Solomon decoder for decoded quantum interferometry cuts gate count about 12-fold versus Google's construction and reserves 19 qubits instead of a 665-qubit worst case. Divsteps identities were proved in Lean and the decoder's failure rate measured at about 1 in 10,000 over 403 million samples. Researchers note Claude tended to be conservative and needed prompting to explore distinct approaches in parallel.

  • Closed-loop design: propose, auto-evaluate, revise, no human in the loop
  • 17x fewer gates on EC point addition, 12x on Reed-Solomon decoding
  • Gains came from new structural insight, not parameter tuning

You could have invented transformers from memory: linear layers, MLPs and attention as associative memories, with softmax giving ~1M memories at d=64

●●●●○ 4/5

Alex Wang's post, 506 points on Hacker News, derives the transformer stack from one idea: every layer is an associative memory that stores key-value pairs and recalls a value by similarity to a query. Recall quality is the ratio of interference from other memories to the target signal, and capacity is where that ratio hits one. A plain outer-product store holds about as many memories as the key dimension; least-squares fitting roughly doubles it; nonlinear gating such as squared ReLU, which suppresses weakly similar keys, pushes it to about 2,700, which is why MLPs store more than a linear map. Swapping the power for an exponential yields softmax attention, whose capacity grows exponentially with its sharpness, reaching about a million memories at 64 dimensions. Attention is then a non-parametric memory where each context token adds one entry, and scaling the softmax sharpness with context length keeps recall reliable at long contexts.

  • One lens (interference vs signal) explains linear, MLP and attention capacity
  • Nonlinearity exists to suppress interference from unrelated keys
  • Softmax sharpness is the knob for long-context recall
